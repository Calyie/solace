# Sources for the service playbooks

The research behind each playbook in prompts/services/ (TASKLIST A2). Every citation was checked against the
publisher, PubMed, the journal or NICE on 2026-11-07; six details were corrected in that check (two journals, one
paper, one edition, one book's editors, one term). Where the evidence is weaker than the playbook's wording might
suggest, it says so; the playbooks were softened to match (procrastination, regret, loneliness, self-compassion).

The playbooks hold methods only, so the writer's prompt stays short; the sources live here.

## Depression and low mood
- Dimidjian S, Hollon SD, Dobson KS, et al. (2006). Randomized trial of behavioral activation, cognitive therapy, and
  antidepressant medication in the acute treatment of adults with major depression. J Consult Clin Psychol 74(4),
  658-670. In more severe depression, behavioural activation did as well as medication and better than cognitive
  therapy.
- Ekers D, Webster L, Van Straten A, et al. (2014). Behavioural activation for depression; an update of meta-analysis
  of effectiveness and sub group analysis. PLoS ONE 9(6), e100100. 26 trials: behavioural activation works.
- NICE (2022). Depression in adults: treatment and management, NG222. Behavioural activation and CBT among the first
  choices.

## Anxiety and worry
- Dugas MJ, Brillon P, Savard P, et al. (2010). A randomized clinical trial of cognitive-behavioral therapy and
  applied relaxation for adults with generalized anxiety disorder. Behavior Therapy 41(1), 46-58. CBT aimed at
  intolerance of uncertainty works; about level with applied relaxation, still improving at two years.
- Wells A (2009). Metacognitive Therapy for Anxiety and Depression. Guilford. Beliefs that worry is useful start it;
  beliefs that it is uncontrollable and dangerous keep it going.
- NICE (2011). Generalised anxiety disorder and panic disorder in adults, CG113. Offer CBT or applied relaxation.

## Overthinking, rumination and obsessive thinking
- Watkins ER, Nolen-Hoeksema S (2014). A habit-goal framework of depressive rumination. J Abnorm Psychol 123(1),
  24-34. Rumination becomes a mental habit set off by cues in the person's context; changing the response to the cue,
  or the cue, breaks it (checked 2026-11-07).
- Watkins ER (2016). Rumination-Focused Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy for Depression. Guilford. Functional analysis,
  if-then plans for the cues, concrete thinking, absorption, compassion.
- Watkins ER, Mullan E, Wingrove J, et al. (2011). Rumination-focused cognitive-behavioural therapy for residual
  depression: phase II randomised controlled trial. Br J Psychiatry 199(4), 317-322. Small trial; it helped, through
  less rumination.
- Watkins ER (2008). Constructive and unconstructive repetitive thought. Psychol Bull 134(2), 163-206. Abstract,
  judging thought about problems is the unhelpful kind; concrete thought is more constructive.

## Panic
- Clark DM (1986). A cognitive approach to panic. Behav Res Ther 24(4), 461-470. Panic comes from reading body
  sensations as a catastrophe.
- Craske MG, Barlow DH (2022). Mastery of Your Anxiety and Panic: Therapist Guide, 5th ed. Oxford University Press.
  Bringing on the feared sensations on purpose (interoceptive exposure) is a core part.

## Social anxiety
- Clark DM, Wells A (1995). A cognitive model of social phobia. In Heimberg RG et al. (eds), Social Phobia, 69-93.
  Guilford. Self-focused attention, safety behaviours and the replay afterwards keep it going.
- Mayo-Wilson E, Dias S, Mavranezouli I, et al. (2014). Psychological and pharmacological interventions for social
  anxiety disorder in adults: a systematic review and network meta-analysis. Lancet Psychiatry 1(5), 368-376.
  Individual CBT had the largest effect of all treatments.
- NICE (2013). Social anxiety disorder, CG159. Individual CBT based on the Clark and Wells (or Heimberg) model.

## Health anxiety
- Tyrer P, Cooper S, Salkovskis P, et al. (2014). Clinical and cost-effectiveness of cognitive behaviour therapy for
  health anxiety in medical patients: a multicentre randomised controlled trial. Lancet 383(9913), 219-225.
- Cooper K, Gregory JD, Walker I, Lambe S, Salkovskis PM (2017). Cognitive behaviour therapy for health anxiety: a
  systematic review and meta-analysis. Behav Cogn Psychother 45(2), 110-123. Large effect, lasting a year.
- Salkovskis PM, Warwick HMC (1986). Morbid preoccupations, health anxiety and reassurance. Behav Res Ther 24(5),
  597-602. Reassurance brings short relief and keeps the anxiety going (a theory paper with two cases).

## Fears and phobias
- Craske MG, Treanor M, Conway CC, Zbozinek T, Vervliet B (2014). Maximizing exposure therapy: an inhibitory learning
  approach. Behav Res Ther 58, 10-23. Test expectations, vary practice, drop safety signals.
- Wolitzky-Taylor KB, Horowitz JD, Powers MB, Telch MJ (2008). Psychological approaches in the treatment of specific
  phobias: a meta-analysis. Clin Psychol Rev 28(6), 1021-1037. Exposure does better than other approaches.
- Öst L-G (1989). One-session treatment for specific phobias. Behav Res Ther 27(1), 1-7 (a case series).

## OCD and obsessive thinking
- Foa EB, Liebowitz MR, Kozak MJ, et al. (2005). Randomized, placebo-controlled trial of exposure and ritual
  prevention, clomipramine, and their combination. Am J Psychiatry 162(1), 151-161. Exposure and response prevention
  did better than clomipramine alone.
- NICE (2005). Obsessive-compulsive disorder and body dysmorphic disorder, CG31. CBT with exposure and response
  prevention (an update is expected in 2027).
- Salkovskis PM (1985). Obsessional-compulsive problems: a cognitive-behavioural analysis. Behav Res Ther 23(5),
  571-583. Inflated responsibility turns ordinary intrusions into obsessions.

## ADHD skills
- Safren SA, Sprich S, Mimiaga MJ, et al. (2010). Cognitive behavioral therapy vs relaxation with educational support
  for medication-treated adults with ADHD and persistent symptoms. JAMA 304(8), 875-880.
- Solanto MV, Marks DJ, Wasserstein J, et al. (2010). Efficacy of meta-cognitive therapy for adult ADHD. Am J
  Psychiatry 167(8), 958-968. Time management, organisation and planning skills improved inattention.
- NICE (2018, updated 2019). ADHD: diagnosis and management, NG87. Psychological support including CBT has a role for
  adults; medication remains first.

## Focus and procrastination
- Gollwitzer PM, Sheeran P (2006). Implementation intentions and goal achievement: a meta-analysis. Adv Exp Soc
  Psychol 38, 69-119. If-then plans: a medium-to-large effect.
- Rozental A, Forsell E, Svensson A, Andersson G, Carlbring P (2015). Internet-based cognitive-behavior therapy for
  procrastination: a randomized controlled trial. J Consult Clin Psychol 83(4), 808-824.
- Wohl MJA, Pychyl TA, Bennett SH (2010). I forgive myself, now I can study. Pers Individ Dif 48(7), 803-808.
  Self-forgiveness was linked with less procrastination later (correlational).
- Sirois F, Pychyl T (2013). Procrastination and the priority of short-term mood regulation. Soc Personal Psychol
  Compass 7(2), 115-127 (theory).

- Dunlosky J, Rawson KA, Marsh EJ, Nathan MJ, Willingham DT (2013). Improving students' learning with effective
  learning techniques. Psychol Sci Public Interest 14(1), 4-58. Practice testing and distributed practice rated
  high utility; rereading and highlighting low (checked 2026-11-07, for learning and remembering what they study).
- Roediger HL, Karpicke JD (2006). Test-enhanced learning: taking memory tests improves long-term retention. Psychol
  Sci 17(3), 249-255. Testing beat restudying on delayed tests.

## Daydreaming
- Somer E (2002). Maladaptive daydreaming: a qualitative inquiry. J Contemp Psychother 32(2-3), 197-212.
- Herscu O, Somer E, Federman A, Soffer-Dudek N (2023). Mindfulness meditation and self-monitoring reduced
  maladaptive daydreaming symptoms: a randomized controlled trial of a brief self-guided web-based program. J Consult
  Clin Psychol 91(5), 285-300.
- Somer E, Lehrfeld J, Bigelsen J, Jopp DS (2016). Development and validation of the Maladaptive Daydreaming Scale.
  Conscious Cogn 39, 77-91 (movement while daydreaming is one of its factors; music items were added in the MDS-16).

## Habits
- Lally P, van Jaarsveld CHM, Potts HWW, Wardle J (2010). How are habits formed. Eur J Soc Psychol 40(6), 998-1009.
  Median 66 days, range 18 to 254 (from the 39 people whose data fitted); one missed day did not matter.
- Gardner B, Lally P, Wardle J (2012). Making health habitual. Br J Gen Pract 62(605), 664-666. Repeat a simple
  action in the same context.

## Sleep
- Qaseem A, Kansagara D, Forciea MA, et al. (2016). Management of chronic insomnia disorder in adults (ACP
  guideline). Ann Intern Med 165(2), 125-133. CBT for insomnia first.
- Edinger JD, Arnedt JT, Bertisch SM, et al. (2021). Behavioral and psychological treatments for chronic insomnia
  disorder in adults (AASM guideline). J Clin Sleep Med 17(2), 255-262. Sleep hygiene alone not recommended.
- Trauer JM, Qian MY, Doyle JS, Rajaratnam SMW, Cunnington D (2015). Cognitive behavioral therapy for chronic
  insomnia: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Ann Intern Med 163(3), 191-204.

## Loss and grief
- Stroebe M, Schut H (1999). The dual process model of coping with bereavement. Death Studies 23(3), 197-224.
- Stroebe M, Schut H, Boerner K (2017). Cautioning health-care professionals: bereaved persons are misguided through
  the stages of grief. Omega 74(4), 455-473. Stage theory has no evidence behind it.
- Klass D, Silverman PR, Nickman SL (eds) (1996). Continuing Bonds: New Understandings of Grief. Taylor & Francis.
- Shear K, Frank E, Houck PR, Reynolds CF (2005). Treatment of complicated grief: a randomized controlled trial.
  JAMA 293(21), 2601-2608.

## Regret
- Zhang JW, Chen S (2016). Self-compassion promotes personal improvement from regret experiences via acceptance.
  Pers Soc Psychol Bull 42(2), 244-258.
- Roese NJ, Summerville A (2005). What we regret most... and why. Pers Soc Psychol Bull 31(9), 1273-1285. The
  biggest regrets lie where there is most opportunity to change.
- Not used: Gilovich and Medvec (1995) on regrets of inaction lasting longest; a 2022 replication did not support it.

## Letting go
- Wade NG, Hoyt WT, Kidwell JEM, Worthington EL (2014). Efficacy of psychotherapeutic interventions to promote
  forgiveness: a meta-analysis. J Consult Clin Psychol 82(1), 154-170.
- Worthington EL (2003). Forgiving and Reconciling: Bridges to Wholeness and Hope. InterVarsity Press (the REACH
  steps).
- Enright RD, Fitzgibbons RP (2015). Forgiveness Therapy. APA. Forgiving is not excusing, forgetting or reconciling.

## Being enough
- Gilbert P (2009). Introducing compassion-focused therapy. Adv Psychiatr Treat 15(3), 199-208.
- Kirby JN, Tellegen CL, Steindl SR (2017). A meta-analysis of compassion-based interventions. Behav Ther 48(6),
  778-792.
- Neff KD, Germer CK (2013). A pilot study and randomized controlled trial of the Mindful Self-Compassion program.
  J Clin Psychol 69(1), 28-44.
- Fennell MJV (1997). Low self-esteem: a cognitive perspective. Behav Cogn Psychother 25(1), 1-26.
- Shafran R, Cooper Z, Fairburn CG (2002). Clinical perfectionism: a cognitive-behavioural analysis. Behav Res Ther
  40(7), 773-791.

- Bandura A (1977). Self-efficacy: toward a unifying theory of behavioral change. Psychol Rev 84(2), 191-215.
  Performance accomplishments (mastery experiences) are the most dependable source of self-efficacy (checked
  2026-11-07, for building self-confidence by doing).

## Relationships and attachment
- Linehan MM (2015). DBT Skills Training Manual, 2nd ed. Guilford (DEAR MAN, GIVE, FAST).
- Carrère S, Gottman JM (1999). Predicting divorce among newlyweds from the first three minutes of a marital conflict
  discussion. Family Process 38(3), 293-301; Gottman JM, Coan J, Carrère S, Swanson C (1998). J Marriage Fam 60(1),
  5-22 (soft start and calming during conflict; the methods were later criticised).
- Mikulincer M, Shaver PR (2016). Attachment in Adulthood, 2nd ed. Guilford.

## Strong emotions and urges
- Linehan MM (2015), as above.
- Storebø OJ, Stoffers-Winterling JM, Völlm BA, et al. (2020). Psychological therapies for people with borderline
  personality disorder. Cochrane Database Syst Rev 5, CD012955. DBT reduced self-harm and severity (low-certainty
  evidence).
- Bowen S, Marlatt GA (2009). Surfing the urge. Psychol Addict Behav 23(4), 666-671 (a seven-day study in smokers).

## Gambling and spending
- NICE (2025). Gambling-related harms: identification, assessment and management, NG248. Gambling-specific CBT (group
  first, individual where a group does not suit) to reduce gambling severity and frequency; motivational interviewing
  to build confidence and commitment; support with debt and other harms; relapse can bring a risk of suicide or
  self-harm (checked 2026-11-07).
- Petry NM, Ginley MK, Rash CJ (2017). A systematic review of treatments for problem gambling. Psychol Addict Behav
  31(8), 951-961. CBT the best supported; brief and motivational approaches help some; therapist contact matters
  more for severe gambling (checked 2026-11-07).
- Hague B, Hall J, Kellett S (2016). Treatments for compulsive buying: a systematic review of the quality,
  effectiveness and progression of the outcome evidence. J Behav Addict 5(3), 379-394. Group CBT the best supported,
  from small studies (checked 2026-11-07).

## Overeating and binge eating
- NICE (2017). Eating disorders: recognition and treatment, NG69. Binge-eating-disorder-focused guided self-help with
  CBT materials first; group CBT for eating disorders next; weight loss is not a therapy target in itself (checked
  2026-11-07).
- Fairburn CG (2013). Overcoming Binge Eating, 2nd ed. Guilford. Self-monitoring, regular eating, alternatives to
  bingeing, problem solving, dieting rules loosened, relapse prevention.
- Bowen S, Marlatt A (2009). Surfing the urge: brief mindfulness-based intervention for college student smokers.
  Psychol Addict Behav 23(4), 666-671. Urge surfing cut smoking without making the urges weaker (used here for any
  urge; checked 2026-11-07).

## Change and ambivalence
- Miller WR, Rollnick S (2023). Motivational Interviewing: Helping People Change and Grow, 4th ed. Guilford.
- Lundahl BW, Kunz C, Brownell C, Tollefson D, Burke BL (2010). A meta-analysis of motivational interviewing. Res
  Soc Work Pract 20(2), 137-160. Small lasting effects; level with other active treatments.

## Career and life coaching
- Whitmore J, revised by Gaskell T (2024). Coaching for Performance, 6th ed. John Murray Business (GROW).
- Theeboom T, Beersma B, van Vianen AEM (2014). Does coaching work? A meta-analysis. J Posit Psychol 9(1), 1-18.
- Wrzesniewski A, Dutton JE (2001). Crafting a job. Acad Manage Rev 26(2), 179-201 (theory).

## Wheel of life
- Byrne U (2005). Wheel of life: effective steps for stress management. Bus Inf Rev 22(2), 123-130. A descriptive
  article. No trial or validation of the wheel exists; it is used here as a coaching tool for structured reflection,
  not as a research-backed method.

## Purpose and ikigai
- Kamiya M (1966). Ikigai ni tsuite (On ikigai). Misuzu Shobō.
- Sone T, Nakaya N, Ohmori K, et al. (2008). Sense of life worth living (ikigai) and mortality in Japan: Ohsaki
  Study. Psychosom Med 70(6), 709-715 (observational).
- Winn M (2014). What is your ikigai? (blog). The four-circle diagram is Zuzunaga's purpose diagram with one word
  changed.
- King LA (2001). The health benefits of writing about life goals. Pers Soc Psychol Bull 27(7), 798-807 (best
  possible self).

## Higher sense
- Pargament KI (2007). Spiritually Integrated Psychotherapy. Guilford.
- Captari LE, Hook JN, Hoyt W, et al. (2018). Integrating clients' religion and spirituality within psychotherapy: a
  comprehensive meta-analysis. J Clin Psychol 74(11), 1938-1951. As effective as standard therapy, with more
  spiritual wellbeing.

## Joy and doing what they love
- Seligman MEP, Steen TA, Park N, Peterson C (2005). Positive psychology progress. Am Psychol 60(5), 410-421 (a
  later replication found a positive placebo did about as well).
- Bolier L, Haverman M, Westerhof GJ, et al. (2013). Positive psychology interventions: a meta-analysis. BMC Public
  Health 13, 119. Small effects.
- Bryant FB, Veroff J (2007). Savoring. Erlbaum.

## Living with illness or pain
- McCracken LM, Vowles KE (2014). Acceptance and commitment therapy and mindfulness for chronic pain. Am Psychol
  69(2), 178-187.
- Hughes LS, Clark J, Colclough JA, Dale E, McMillan D (2017). ACT for chronic pain: a systematic review and
  meta-analyses. Clin J Pain 33(6), 552-568. Better functioning and mood, not less pain.
- NICE (2021). Chronic pain (primary and secondary), NG193. Consider ACT or CBT for chronic primary pain.

## Loneliness
- Masi CM, Chen H-Y, Hawkley LC, Cacioppo JT (2011). A meta-analysis of interventions to reduce loneliness. Pers Soc
  Psychol Rev 15(3), 219-266. Work on unhelpful social thinking had the largest effect.
- Käll A, Jägholm S, Hesser H, et al. (2020). Internet-based cognitive behavior therapy for loneliness: a pilot
  randomized controlled trial. Behav Ther 51(1), 54-68.

## Stress and burnout
- Sonnentag S, Fritz C (2007). The Recovery Experience Questionnaire. J Occup Health Psychol 12(3), 204-221.
- Maslach C, Leiter MP (2016). Understanding the burnout experience. World Psychiatry 15(2), 103-111.

- World Health Organization (2019). Burn-out an "occupational phenomenon": International Classification of
  Diseases (ICD-11, QD85). A syndrome from chronic workplace stress not successfully managed: exhaustion, mental
  distance or cynicism, reduced professional efficacy; an occupational phenomenon, not a medical condition (checked
  2026-11-07).

## The approaches and the safety plan
- Hofmann SG, Asnaani A, Vonk IJJ, Sawyer AT, Fang A (2012). The efficacy of cognitive behavioral therapy: a review
  of meta-analyses. Cogn Ther Res 36(5), 427-440.
- A-Tjak JGL, Davis ML, Morina N, et al. (2015). A meta-analysis of the efficacy of acceptance and commitment
  therapy. Psychother Psychosom 84(1), 30-36.
- Kuyken W, Warren FC, Taylor RS, et al. (2016). Efficacy of mindfulness-based cognitive therapy in prevention of
  depressive relapse: an individual patient data meta-analysis. JAMA Psychiatry 73(6), 565-574.
- Stanley B, Brown GK (2012). Safety Planning Intervention. Cogn Behav Pract 19(2), 256-264.
