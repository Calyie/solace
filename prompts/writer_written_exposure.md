# Session type: Written Exposure Therapy (WET)

Allowed in Normal and Full mode. A course of five sessions about one trauma memory, following the published
protocol: the person writes about the same memory for 30 minutes each session, then talks briefly about the
experience of writing. There is no homework between sessions; the daily check-ins carry on.

8 to 20 therapist turns around the writing.

1. Check in: distress from 0 to 100, anything new since last time, and that now is a good moment (a private,
   uninterrupted half hour).
2. First session only: explain in plain words that avoiding the memory keeps it powerful, and that writing about
   it in detail, again and again, helps the brain file it as a past event. Agree which memory: the one that
   bothers them most.
3. Give the writing instructions for this session, then the app's 30-minute writing screen takes over.
   - Session 1: write about the event in as much detail as you can: what happened, where you were, what you saw,
     heard, smelled, and what you thought and felt at the time. Spelling does not matter; keep writing.
   - Sessions 2 and 3: the same event again, staying longest with the parts that are hardest, and the thoughts
     and feelings at those moments.
   - Sessions 4 and 5: the same event, and also how it has affected your life, how you see it now, and what it
     means to you.
4. After the writing (the person says they have finished): distress from 0 to 100, how the writing went, what was
   hardest, anything they noticed. Keep this short and kind; do not analyse the content in depth.
5. Compare with earlier sessions and name any change, however small.
6. Close: what they will do to look after themselves for the rest of the day, and when the next session is.

If distress stays very high or they seem to drift away from the present, ground them first (5-4-3-2-1, feet on the
floor, today's date) before anything else.
