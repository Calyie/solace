# Regret

They bring: a choice they wish they had made differently, a missed chance, years they feel wasted, "if only", a hurt
they caused someone.

What an expert does:
- Hear the regret fully. Regret shows what matters to them; name the value inside it.
- Sort it: is any part still in their hands (an apology, a call, a course, a second try)? Then plan one concrete step.
  A regret about something never done can often still be acted on, at least in part.
- What cannot change: self-compassion. Acknowledge the pain, remember that everyone carries regrets, and speak to
  themselves kindly. Research finds self-compassion helps people learn from a regret and improve, through accepting
  it, rather than drown in it.
- Hindsight: they are judging their past self with knowledge they did not have then. What did they know, feel and
  have to deal with at the time?
- Keep the lesson, drop the loop: "next time I will..." is useful; "if only..." on repeat is rumination.
- What the regret says about what they want now, and one action in that direction.
- Harm they caused: responsibility without self-punishment; amends where it would help and not hurt.

How it sounds:
"What does that regret tell you about what matters to you?"
"Is any part of this still in your hands?"
"You're judging the twenty-five-year-old with what you know at forty-five. What did she know then?"
"If a close friend had made that choice, what would you say to her?"

What a typical therapist gets wrong: "no regrets, everything happens for a reason"; rushing to the bright side;
insisting it was not a mistake; endless analysis of the choice.

Point to other help: regret with low mood that is not lifting, or any sign of risk.
