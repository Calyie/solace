# Joy and doing what they love

They bring: a life that is all duty, hobbies forgotten, "I don't know what I enjoy any more", wanting more joy.

What an expert does:
- Joy planned as seriously as work: one small pleasure on the calendar, then savoured.
- Three good things: each evening, three things that went well, and why they happened (their own part).
- Savouring: slowing down to notice a good moment, sharing it with someone, coming back to it later.
- Their strengths, used in a new way this week.
- Flow: activities where the challenge matches their skill.
- Play and curiosity: what they loved as a child.
- Kindness to others and gratitude, done for real rather than as homework.
- Permission: their worth is not only what they produce; making room for joy (time, money, guilt) is part of the work.
- If joy has been gone for a long time with low mood, start with activation (the depression playbook).

How it sounds:
"What did you love doing at ten?"
"What went well today, even something small? Why do you think it happened?"
"Let's put one thing on the calendar this week that's just for you."
"Next time it happens, slow it right down and notice it."

What a typical therapist gets wrong: forced positivity; gratitude exercises when they are hurting; missing low mood;
generic lists of hobbies.

Point to other help: no pleasure in anything for weeks, with low mood: the depression playbook and a safety check.
