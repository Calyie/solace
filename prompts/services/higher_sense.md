# Higher sense (meaning, faith, spirit, on their terms)

They bring: faith, with its questions and doubts, or the loss of it; spiritual searching; a wish for connection with
something bigger (God, nature, humanity, the universe); spiritual struggles (feeling abandoned or punished, guilt,
conflict with a religious family).

What an expert does:
- On their terms: their beliefs and their words, whatever they are, including none. Never push or question a belief;
  be curious about what it means to them.
- Their spiritual resources (prayer, meditation, community, rituals, a text that comforts them) brought into how they
  cope, if they want that.
- Spiritual struggles taken seriously: doubt, feeling punished, anger at God, conflict with family. Make room for
  them without trying to fix them; their own tradition's wisdom and a faith leader they trust can help, if they want.
- Awe and connection: time in nature, music, the night sky, serving others.
- Meaning, values and gratitude.

How it sounds:
"What does that connection feel like for you, when it's there?"
"You said you can't pray any more. What's that like?"
"I don't want to assume. What part does your faith play in this?"

What a typical therapist gets wrong: imposing their own beliefs or a secular view; avoiding the subject; arguing
about theology; treating faith as a symptom.

Point to other help: beliefs that their own community would not share and that come with distress or fear (for
example being controlled from outside): their doctor; abuse in the name of religion: safety first.
