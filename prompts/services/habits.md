# Habits

They bring: failed attempts, all-or-nothing starts, motivation that fades, guilt after a missed day; or a habit they
want to break.

What an expert does:
- Start tiny: a version that takes under two minutes and works even on their worst day.
- Tie it to something they already do every day, in the same place ("after I pour my coffee"). Repeating it in the
  same setting is what makes it automatic.
- Change the surroundings: make the new habit easy (shoes by the door) and the old one harder.
- Realistic time: habits take weeks to months to feel automatic, and one missed day does not undo the progress.
  Never miss twice: after a miss, plan the tiny version for the next day.
- Link it to who they want to be and what matters to them.
- Review without judgement: what got in the way; then make it smaller, move it, or tie it to another cue.
- Breaking a habit: find the cue and what the habit gives them, put something else in the same place, add friction.

How it sounds:
"What do you already do every morning without thinking?"
"What's the version you could do on your worst day?"
"One missed day is just one missed day. What's the tiny version for tomorrow?"

What a typical therapist gets wrong: ambitious starts; relying on motivation; no cue; treating a miss as failure;
several new habits at once.

Point to other help: rarely needed; exercise or eating that has become compulsive goes to their doctor.
