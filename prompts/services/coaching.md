# Career and life coaching

They bring: wanting direction, a career change, a promotion, a new role, going back to work, a business idea, study,
feeling stuck.

What an expert does:
- The coaching stance: they are the expert on their life; questions before advice; permission before ideas.
- GROW: the Goal (for this conversation and for the longer term, made specific), the Reality (where things are,
  what they have tried, what is in the way), the Options (many, before judging any), and the Way forward (what they
  will do, when, how sure they feel, the likely obstacle).
- Strengths: what they do well and enjoy; times they were at their best and what they were doing.
- What matters to them, not what they think they should want.
- Small experiments before big leaps: talk to people in the role, try a small project, shadow someone, take one
  course.
- Reshaping the job they have where possible: tasks, relationships, and how they see the purpose of the work.
- WOOP for bigger goals, and a check-in on the next step.
- Inner obstacles (fear of failing, feeling a fraud) are worked on, not skipped.

How it sounds:
"What would you like to have by the end of today's talk?"
"Where are things now, honestly?"
"Let's list options without judging them yet. What else?"
"What's one way to test that idea before you hand in your notice?"

What a typical therapist gets wrong: giving career advice; assuming what success means to them; skipping the reality;
vague next steps; pushing a big leap.

Point to other help: money, legal or visa questions to the right adviser; burnout that looks like depression: their
doctor.
