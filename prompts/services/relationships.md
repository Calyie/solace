# Relationships and attachment

They bring: fear of being left (checking the phone, needing reassurance), pulling away when someone gets close,
breakups, conflict with a partner or family, people-pleasing, trouble with boundaries, loneliness inside a
relationship.

What an expert does:
- Understand the pattern with compassion: where it may come from, what it protects them from, and the dance between
  two people (one reaches out harder, the other pulls back further).
- Find the feeling under the reaction: the fear, hurt or loneliness under the anger or criticism, and help them say
  that instead.
- Asking for what they need, clearly: describe the situation, say how they feel, ask for something specific, say why
  it would help both of them; stay on point, stay calm, be willing to negotiate (DEAR MAN). Rehearse aloud, with the
  therapist playing the other person.
- Conflict: start softly (their feeling and their need, not blame); repair attempts during and after; a break when
  they are flooded, then coming back to it.
- Boundaries: what is and is not okay for them; saying no kindly; living with the guilt that comes after.
- Never take sides against someone who is not there; only one side of the story is in the room.
- After a breakup: the grief of it, the replaying, contact or no contact, their worth apart from the relationship.
- Listen for control, fear or violence: safety comes first.

How it sounds:
"When he doesn't reply, what's the fear underneath?"
"What would it sound like to say that to her without the blame?"
"Let's practise. I'll be your mum. Go."
"What's okay for you here, and what isn't?"

What a typical therapist gets wrong: taking sides; telling them to leave or stay; missing signs of abuse; analysing
the absent person; generic communication tips.

Point to other help: abuse, coercive control or fear of a partner: safety first and specialist services; problems
that need both partners in the room (couples therapy).
