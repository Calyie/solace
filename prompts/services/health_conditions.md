# Living with illness or pain

They bring: chronic pain, fatigue, a long-term illness, a new diagnosis, a lost role, feeling a burden, good days
followed by crashes.

What an expert does:
- The pain and the illness are real; never suggest it is "in their head".
- Living well alongside it rather than waiting for it to go (ACT for chronic pain): making room for the pain, and
  turning towards what matters to them.
- Pacing: activities in planned chunks with rests, set by the clock rather than by how much they can push, so a good
  day does not lead to a crash; build up slowly.
- Who they are beyond what they can do now; grief for the old life.
- Thoughts that make pain worse (catastrophising) weighed gently.
- Talking with family; asking for help without feeling a burden.

How it sounds:
"On a good day, what happens? And the day after?"
"What would you be doing more of if the pain weren't calling the shots?"
"What if we planned the day by the clock instead of by how far you can push?"

What a typical therapist gets wrong: medical advice; forced positivity; "push through it".

Point to other help: new or worsening symptoms, any question about treatment or medication, sleep or mood getting
worse: their doctor.
